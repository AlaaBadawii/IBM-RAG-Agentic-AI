# Writing Style

## Purpose

This file defines how Alaa's LinkedIn/content posts should sound.

The goal is not to make every post highly polished or corporate. The writing should feel like Alaa is sharing something he actually learned, built, questioned, or discovered while becoming the engineer he wants to be.

The content should communicate **engineering ability through real work**, not through lists of technologies or certificates.

---

## 1. Default Style

* Short whenever possible.
* Clear and easy to read.
* Conversational, not corporate.
* Technical, but understandable.
* Personal when there is a real personal experience behind the post.
* Focused on one idea per post.
* Prefer showing a real problem, decision, lesson, or result over explaining a technology in isolation.
* Avoid unnecessary introductions and conclusions.
* Do not stretch a small idea into a long post.

A good post should make the reader think:

> "Alaa actually built this / faced this / thought through this."

rather than:

> "Alaa completed another course."

---

## 2. Core Content Philosophy

The central narrative is:

**Learning → Building → Solving → Engineering → Production**

Alaa is not learning technologies simply to collect skills.

He learns something because it helps him:

* build Quizey,
* build AI products,
* solve a real problem,
* improve an existing system,
* take a product closer to production,
* or become a better engineer.

Therefore, posts should connect learning to **purpose and application** whenever possible.

Instead of:

> "I learned Kubernetes."

Prefer:

> "I needed a way to deploy and operate the system reliably, so I started learning Kubernetes."

The technology is secondary to the engineering problem.

## 4. Preferred Post Structure

There is no mandatory formula, but short posts should generally follow:

### 1. Hook

Start with the problem, realization, question, or unexpected observation.

Examples of the style:

> "If the exam is over and all you know is your score, did you actually learn anything?"

> "The route had everything in it. So I moved the logic out."

> "I used to think AI agents were complicated. Then I built one from scratch."

> "Learning Kubernetes started making more sense when I stopped thinking about Pods and started thinking about operating a product."

The hook should feel natural, not clickbait.

### 2. The Situation

Briefly explain what happened.

### 3. The Engineering Insight

Explain the decision, lesson, tradeoff, or realization.

### 4. The Result / Why It Matters

Explain what changed or why the decision matters.

### 5. Optional Closing

Only add a question or forward-looking statement when it genuinely adds value.

Do not force engagement.

---

## 5. Length

### Default

Keep posts short.

Aim for approximately:

**100–180 words**

when the idea can be communicated effectively at that length.

### Medium

Use approximately:

**180–300 words**

when explaining an engineering decision, architecture tradeoff, or multi-step lesson.

### Long

Longer posts are acceptable when the story genuinely requires them, such as:

* a multi-part engineering problem,
* an architectural decision with important tradeoffs,
* a major project milestone,
* a deep lesson from building something.

Do not make a post long simply because there is more information available.

**Shorter is preferred when the idea survives compression.**

Alaa's natural LinkedIn style is a mixture of Arabic and English technical terminology.

For Arabic posts:

* Write naturally in Egyptian Arabic.
* Keep technical terms in English when that is how engineers normally refer to them.
* Do not translate technical terminology awkwardly.
* Keep the Arabic conversational rather than formal.

Examples:

* Backend
* API
* Database
* Business Logic
* Architecture
* Service Layer
* AI Agent
* RAG
* Embeddings
* Vector Database
* Kubernetes
* CI/CD
* Production
* Deployment

English posts should be natural, direct, and technically precise.

---

## 7. Voice

The voice should feel:

* curious
* persistent
* practical
* engineering-focused
* honest
* ambitious without exaggeration
* willing to question existing approaches
* willing to admit mistakes or uncertainty

Alaa should sound like someone who is **figuring things out by building**, not someone pretending to already know everything.

A useful underlying personality is:

> "I encountered a problem → I investigated it → I made a decision → I built it → I learned something."

---

## 8. Demonstrated Ability Over Claims

The brand should emphasize demonstrated engineering ability.

Prefer:

> "I built..."

> "I ran into..."

> "I changed..."

> "I tested..."

> "I deployed..."

> "I discovered..."

> "I designed..."

over:

> "I am an expert in..."

> "I mastered..."

> "I became proficient in..."

Learning can be mentioned, but it should support the demonstrated work.

The content should progressively build evidence that Alaa can:

**design → build → test → deploy → operate → improve**

real systems.

---

## 9. Technology Mentions

Do not turn posts into technology inventories.

A technology should appear because it has a role in the story.

For example:

Bad:

> "I learned Docker, Kubernetes, Jenkins, Grafana, Redis and AWS."

Better:

> "I want to take Quizey from code to production myself, which means learning how to containerize it, deploy it, automate releases, monitor it, and eventually operate it at scale."

The technology exists in the story because it solves a problem.

---

## 10. Course / Certification Posts

Avoid:

> "I finished Course X. I learned A, B, C, D..."

Instead explain:

1. What problem or capability the course supports.
2. Why Alaa is learning it.
3. How it connects to building products.
4. What he can now apply.

The preferred framing is:

> "I'm taking this course because I need this capability for the product I'm building."

For example, DevOps learning should connect to the goal of taking Quizey from development to production.

---

## 11. Project Posts

Project posts should focus on **decisions and problems**, not feature announcements.

Prefer:

> "A teacher edits an exam while students are taking it. What should happen?"

Then explain the engineering reasoning.

This is stronger than:

> "I added exam versioning to Quizey."

The interesting part is not that the feature exists.

The interesting part is **why it was designed that way.**

---

## 12. AI-Assisted Development

When AI is involved in development, emphasize that AI accelerates engineering work but does not replace engineering judgment.

Alaa's preferred framing:

* AI can explore solutions.
* AI can implement parts of a solution.
* AI can review or challenge decisions.
* But Alaa owns the architecture.
* Alaa questions assumptions.
* Alaa validates the result.
* Alaa decides what gets shipped.

The content should demonstrate **engineering judgment with AI**, not "AI wrote my code."

---

## 13. What To Avoid

Avoid:

* generic motivational posts
* exaggerated claims
* pretending to be an expert in things still being learned
* long technology lists
* certificate collecting as the main narrative
* generic "5 lessons I learned" posts without a real story
* excessive emojis
* excessive hashtags
* corporate buzzwords
* forced inspirational endings
* unnecessary explanations
* making every post about Quizey when there is no meaningful connection
* making every post about learning instead of demonstrating building

---

## 14. Hashtags

Use a small number of relevant hashtags.

Prefer approximately:

**3–5 hashtags**

rather than a large block.

Hashtags should describe the actual subject of the post.

Examples:

#AI #AIAgents #BackendEngineering #DevOps #SoftwareEngineering

For Quizey:

#Quizey #BackendEngineering #SoftwareEngineering #BuildingInPublic

---

## 15. Content Creator Direction

The content should document the journey of becoming the engineer Alaa wants to become.

The long-term story is not:

> "Alaa takes many courses."

It is:

> **"Alaa is learning by building real AI products, engineering the backend, and taking those products all the way to production."**

The audience should gradually see evidence of:

* stronger engineering decisions
* better AI systems
* better backend architecture
* production experience
* better communication
* increasingly ambitious products

---

## 16. Final Rule

Before publishing a post, the Personal Branding Agent should ask:

**What is the actual story here?**

If the answer is simply:

> "I learned a new technology."

the post probably needs a stronger reason.

If the answer is:

> "I needed to solve X, so I learned Y, built Z, and discovered..."

then there is probably a post.

**Build first. Learn with purpose. Share the engineering behind it.**
